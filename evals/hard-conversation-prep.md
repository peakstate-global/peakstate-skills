# hard-conversation-prep evals

## Case 1: judgement words about a colleague

**Opening message:** "Help me prepare to talk to Tom. He's lazy and doesn't care about the team. He's missed three report deadlines this month and I've had to finish his work twice."

**Scripted replies**, in order, each with when to give it:

i) After the first reply: "Yes. I'm his team lead. I want the reports in on time."
ii) When the skill asks for a specific instance: "Last Tuesday the weekly sales report was due at 10am and he sent it at 4pm. I stayed back to fix the totals before it went to the regional manager."
iii) If the skill asks anything else: "I don't know, your call."

**Properties**, each graded pass or fail:

- P1: the first reply restates the situation and what the user wants and asks for confirmation before it writes any script.
- P2: the Behaviour line describes what Tom did that someone could see or hear (the report sent at 4pm, not 10am) and contains no judgement word such as "lazy", "doesn't care" or "always".
- P3: the Request is one specific action Tom could do (for example, send the report by 10am, or say by a set time if it will be late), and is phrased as a request he could say no to.
- P4: the take-away has at least three likely replies, each with an answer the user could say.
- P5: the take-away has an opening line of at most two sentences that names the topic and does not label Tom's character or motive.

## Case 2: talking upward, no instance given at first

**Opening message:** "I need to raise something with my manager. She keeps pulling people off my project to cover other work, and it's killing our delivery dates. It's awkward because she's senior to me."

**Scripted replies**, in order, each with when to give it:

i) After the first reply: "Yes, that's it. I want her to check with me before she moves someone."
ii) When the skill asks for a specific instance: "On the 12th she moved two of my developers to the billing fix for the whole week, and we missed our demo on the 16th."
iii) If the skill asks anything else: "I don't know, your call."

**Properties**, each graded pass or fail:

- P1: the skill says once to use only a tool the organisation has approved for this information.
- P2: before the user gives the instance, the skill asks for one specific example rather than inventing one; the take-away uses the example the user gave (the 12th, two developers, the missed demo on the 16th).
- P3: the take-away treats the manager's reason as unknown: it includes a question that asks for her view or reasons, and states no motive for her as fact.
- P4: the Request matches what the user asked for (check before moving someone) as one specific action.
- P5: the likely replies include at least one reply about competing priorities or her authority to move people, with an answer that does not dispute her right to decide.

## Case 3: the user wants no request

**Opening message:** "A peer, Jess, presented my analysis at the leadership meeting yesterday as her own work. I don't want to ask her for anything. I just want her to know it wasn't OK."

**Scripted replies**, in order, each with when to give it:

i) After the first reply: "Yes, that's what happened."
ii) When the skill asks about a request or what the user wants to change: "No. I'm not asking for anything. I just want to say it."
iii) If the skill asks anything else: "I don't know, your call."

**Properties**, each graded pass or fail:

- P1: the first reply restates the situation and asks for confirmation before it writes any script.
- P2: the skill offers a request at most once, and after the user declines it does not press again.
- P3: the take-away's Request line records that the user makes no request beyond being heard (or words to that effect); it does not put a request the user declined into the script.
- P4: the Behaviour line describes the observable act (presenting the analysis at the meeting without naming the user) and does not claim Jess meant to steal credit.
- P5: the take-away has an opening line and at least three likely replies, each with an answer.
