### Possible biases and fallacies

**False cause (fallacy), high confidence**
- Passage: "Our competitor's app went viral a month after they added dark mode, so dark mode drives growth"
- Generous reading: you may know more than the sentence says, such as users or reviews that named dark mode as the reason they shared the app. With that link shown, the timing would be real support.
- Concern: this passage may treat a sequence as a cause. One app, one month apart, leaves many other causes open.
- Checking question: What else changed for the competitor in that month?

**False consensus effect (bias), medium confidence**
- Passage: "Everyone on my team uses dark mode"
- Generous reading: your team may be heavy users of your own app, so their habits are an early signal of what engaged users prefer.
- Concern: this passage may take one group's preference as the customers' preference. Product teams often differ from their users in device, age and working hours.
- Checking question: Have we asked people outside our own team?

**Appeal to ignorance (fallacy), medium confidence**
- Passage: "I have never heard a customer complain about it"
- Generous reading: if customers have many ways to reach you and none raised it, that silence is weak evidence of low risk or low concern.
- Concern: this passage may treat an absence of complaints as evidence for the plan. Customers rarely complain about a feature that does not exist, so silence may also mean they do not care about dark mode.
- Checking question: Did anyone look in a way that would have found demand or objections, such as a search of tickets or a short survey?

**Sunk cost (bias), medium confidence**
- Passage: "We have already spent six months on the redesign, so we cannot pause it now"
- Generous reading: stopping a large piece of work near its end loses momentum and coordination, which is a real future cost and a sound reason to keep going.
- Concern: this passage may give the six months already spent as the reason, when the decision depends on the cost and benefit still to come.
- Checking question: If we were starting today, would we choose to ship this redesign with dark mode in it?

**False dilemma (fallacy), low confidence**
- Passage: "we cannot pause it now, and dark mode should ship with it"
- Generous reading: you may mean that bundling is the cheapest route, because the redesign already touches every screen.
- Concern: this passage may present two options, pause the redesign or ship dark mode inside it, when a third exists: ship the redesign as planned and add dark mode in a later release.
- Checking question: What would it cost to ship dark mode one release after the redesign?

### What would change this reading

Evidence from your own customers that they want dark mode (requests, reviews or survey answers) would make the competitor's story unnecessary, and the pitch would stand on that evidence instead.
