- **Technological:** video calls, cloud tools and shared documents make most desk work possible from anywhere.
- **Economic:** the CBD economy was built on a five-day weekday crowd. Cafes, gyms and retail in the centre depend on office workers, so fewer workers means fewer reasons for anyone else to come.
- **Environmental:** long commutes and crowded transport lost their social licence during the pandemic, and they have not fully regained it.
- **Political:** planning rules zone the centre for offices, so it has few residents to fill it at night and on weekends.

Incentives that keep the issue in place:

- Landlords hold vacant space at the book rent rather than cut rents, because a lower rent would devalue the whole building on paper.
- Workers save commute time and money on each day at home, and they keep that saving.
- Converting offices to homes is slow and costly, so owners wait instead of acting.

The usual fix at this layer: rezone the centre for mixed use, subsidise office-to-residential conversion, and fund transport and events to bring people back.

**Systemic causes, thread 2: "Back to the office"**
