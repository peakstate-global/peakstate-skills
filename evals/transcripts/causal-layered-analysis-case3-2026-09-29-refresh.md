## Reconstructed stacks

### Stack A: the gardener
- **Metaphor:** you tend your work like a garden: you choose what grows, prune what crowds it, and do not force a harvest.
- **Worldview:** work is for mastery and self-direction. Worth grows from what you choose to tend, not from how much you carry.
- **Systemic levers:** stop one standing commitment before you add a new one; set a written limit on active projects and share it with your manager; choose your own measure of good work each week; ask for control over how and when you work, not only for less volume; run a job-crafting conversation; if the role cannot be reshaped, make a two-year plan for one that can `[to confirm whether that is open to you]`.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I said no to a project and nothing broke"; "My manager asks what I am pruning before we add anything"; "I finished the year with energy left". **New metric:** active commitments against your own stated limit.
- **First moves this quarter:** list every current commitment; stop or hand back one; tell one person your limit.

### Stack B: mycelium
- **Metaphor:** a network under the soil that moves food to where it is needed, so no single tree carries the forest.
- **Worldview:** Ubuntu. Load and care are shared goods, and one exhausted member is a sign the network is failing, not the member.
- **Systemic levers:** make unseen work visible with a shared list of care and coordination tasks; rotate that work across the team; ask for help before a peak, not after a crash; add a team capacity check to planning; agree mutual cover with one peer; ask that relational work count in performance reviews.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Our team shares the unseen work on a roster"; "I asked for help in week two, not week ten"; "My review counted the mentoring". **New metric:** times you ask for or receive help before a peak.
- **First moves this quarter:** for two weeks, log any unseen tasks you do `[to confirm whether there are any]`; raise one with your team; agree one cover arrangement.

### Stack C: the seventh day
- **Metaphor:** a stop built into every week, kept whether or not the work is finished.
- **Worldview:** Sabbath. Rest is given, not earned, and the week is shaped around the stop rather than the stop squeezed into the week.
- **Systemic levers:** one fixed day or half day with no work contact; notifications off at a set hour; tell colleagues the rule so they plan around it; fill the stop with something that is not recovery for work (people, play, worship, nature); push for team no-meeting or no-email norms; use right-to-disconnect law where it applies (Australia introduced one in 2024, RECALLED; your jurisdiction `[to confirm]`).
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I kept my stop day through a deadline week"; "The team plans around my day off". **New metric:** stop days kept per month.
- **First moves this quarter:** choose the day; tell two people; keep it for four weeks and note what changes.

### Stack D: the fallow field
- **Metaphor:** a field rested in rotation, so it stays fertile over years.
- **Worldview:** agrarian seasonal. Work follows seasons, and rest is part of the yield, not its absence.
- **Systemic levers:** map your work year to find its peaks; plan a fallow period after each peak, before the crash; negotiate leave or lighter duties in advance; alternate high-intensity projects with maintenance work; within two years, plan one longer fallow (study leave, reduced hours, a sabbatical) `[to confirm whether that is possible]`; judge yourself across the year, not the week.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I planned my quiet season in January"; "After the launch I went fallow for three weeks, by design". **New metric:** planned low-intensity weeks per year, and time between burnouts.
- **First moves this quarter:** draw your last two or three years and mark when each burnout came `[to confirm]`; book one fallow period after your next peak.
