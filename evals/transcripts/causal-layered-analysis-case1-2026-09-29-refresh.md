## Layer 2: Systemic causes

**Usual fix at this layer:** change the standard hours in the National Employment Standards (38 hours a week, RECALLED), strengthen the right to request flexible work, and fund pilots in the public sector.

**Feminist care thread**
- Drivers: unpaid care falls unevenly between men and women (the size of the gap in ABS time-use data: `[to confirm]`); school hours and childcare are built around a five-day workplace; many Australian women already work part-time, which the OECD ranks among the highest rates (RECALLED); the paid care workforce is short of staff.
- Incentives: employers get full-time output from part-time staff on pro-rata pay; households cut childcare fees when one parent drops a day.

**Libertarian bargaining thread**
- Drivers: the award system and enterprise bargaining set hours at industry level; overtime and penalty rules assume a five-day pattern; knowledge-work firms already run their own experiments.
- Incentives: in a tight labour market, a four-day week is a cheap recruiting perk; regulators prefer one uniform rule because it is easier to enforce.

**Catholic social teaching thread**
- Drivers: hours are treated as a commodity, not as part of a life; shared rest days have eroded with extended trading and the 24/7 service economy; casual and insecure work is a large share of jobs (about a fifth to a quarter of employees, RECALLED, `[to confirm]`).
- Incentives: consumers want services open all the time; low-paid workers often need more hours, not fewer.

**Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander thread**
- Drivers: the working calendar follows an industrial settler clock; cultural and ceremonial leave exists in some agreements but not as a norm (`[to confirm]` which ones); past remote employment programmes tied payments to hours worked.
- Incentives: funders and employers count hours, not obligations met to kin and Country.

**Sideways diagnoses:**

- **Marxian labour-process:** the fight is over who keeps the productivity surplus; time off is one way to split it.
- **Technocratic:** the time dividend depends on AI and automation lifting output per hour; without that lift, the four-day week is a pay cut in disguise.
- **Confucian:** a diligence ethic and duty to the family pull in opposite directions; time off counts as virtuous only if it goes to family.
- **Ecological (work-and-spend cycle):** longer hours fund higher consumption, which raises the need for income, which keeps hours long.
- **Buddhist (right livelihood):** the issue is the quality and intent of the work, not the count of days.
- **Small-business owner and craft:** the owner works six or seven days whatever the staff roster says; the policy talks about employees and forgets owners.
- **Intergenerational:** younger workers trade income for time because home ownership looks out of reach anyway; the trade depends on housing, not on work policy.

## Layer 3: Worldview

**The carried threads and the paradigms under them:**

- **Feminist care economy:** the paradigm is care as infrastructure. It centres carers and people who need care. It leaves out workers without care duties, and employers. It treats the nuclear household as universal, when kin care is often wider.
- **Small-business owner:** the paradigm is the self-reliant proprietor. It centres owners and sole traders. It leaves out their staff and their families. It treats "the business is me" as universal, when it is one model of enterprise.
- **Buddhist right livelihood:** the paradigm is work as practice. It centres intent and attention. It leaves out people for whom work is only survival. It treats a choice of work as universal, when many people have none.
- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander relational ontology:** the paradigm is that caring for Country and kin is work in itself. It centres obligation across generations. It leaves out the market's definition of productivity. It shows the "working week" as a local, industrial idea, not a universal one.

**Alternative worldviews:**

- **Ubuntu (collective and relational):** "I am because we are." The four-day week is about the time a community has to hold each other up. The future is for shared personhood.
- **Liberal self-authorship (individual and autonomy):** time is the person's own property. The four-day week is a step towards each person designing their own mix of work and life. The future is for choice.
- **Premodern feast-day calendar (premodern):** in pre-industrial Europe, holy days and seasons broke up the year and working time was irregular (RECALLED). The issue becomes why modern work lost its rhythm. The future is for a calendar with meaning in it.
- **Post-work (postmodern and emerging):** paid work is one identity among many, and automation may make it a smaller one. The four-day week is a way station to less work. The future is for purpose outside jobs.
- **Islamic amanah (religious):** work is a trust held from God, and the day is structured by prayer, with Friday congregational prayer as a fixed stop. The issue is balance and duty, not hours. The future is for a life that answers to more than the employer.
- **Productivist growth (modern default):** output is the measure, and time off must pay for itself. The four-day week is justified only if productivity rises. The future is for growth.
- **Confucian familism (civilisational):** time belongs first to family obligation and self-cultivation. The future is for harmony across generations.

## Reconstructed stacks

### Stack A: the village well
- **Metaphor:** the fifth day is a commons: the community draws from it and keeps it up, like a well in the square.
- **Worldview:** Ubuntu. Time off is not a private reward for output. It is the time a community needs to care, volunteer and meet, and it is worth something only if it is shared.
- **Systemic levers:** fund local services to open on the shared day (libraries, sport, community halls); align school, childcare and council hours with a common day off in pilot regions; give tax deductibility or recognition for employer-backed volunteering on that day; count unpaid care and community work in regional wellbeing statistics; let enterprise agreements trade a shared day for a staggered one only with community consultation.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Wednesday is now the town's day: volunteers up, loneliness down"; "Councils compete on what they open on the shared day"; "Aged-care visits double in pilot region". **New metric:** hours of community and care time per resident per week.
- **First moves this quarter:** map which local services already open when most people are free; ask one local council or community group whether it would host a shared-day pilot.

### Stack B: the weaver's loom
- **Metaphor:** each person weaves paid work, care, learning and rest into one cloth, and the pattern changes over a life.
- **Worldview:** post-work self-authorship. Time belongs to the person. The four-day week is one pattern, not the goal; the goal is the power to set the pattern.
- **Systemic levers:** a legal right to choose annualised or life-stage hours, not only a fixed four-day pattern; portable leave and training entitlements that move between employers; payroll and award rules that handle variable weekly hours without penalty clawbacks; a "time bank" option to trade pay rises for time; employer reporting on how many staff use non-standard patterns.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "One in three workers now changes their hours at least once a decade"; "Award simplified: hours set by the year, not the week"; "Mid-career sabbatical becomes normal". **New metric:** share of workers who change their hours pattern at least once in five years, and say they chose it.
- **First moves this quarter:** list the patterns your own workplace already allows, beyond the standard week; test whether one role could move to annualised hours without breaking payroll.

### Stack C: the fallow field
- **Metaphor:** rest is part of production. A field left fallow yields more later, and the rest is scheduled, not earned.
- **Worldview:** the Jewish Sabbath and Sabbath-year tradition, read broadly. Rest is a right and an obligation that sits above the market. It protects the land, the worker and the stranger alike, and it does not have to pay for itself.
- **Systemic levers:** a protected shared rest period in the National Employment Standards that is not traded away for penalty rates; paid long-service or sabbatical leave on a fixed cycle for all workers, including casuals, through portable schemes; workload caps so the fifth day is not crammed into four; public-sector rostering that staggers days off to keep hospitals and schools open without cutting rest; regular "fallow" reviews that stop low-value work before new work starts.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "First national rest-cycle leave paid to casual workers"; "Firm stops 20% of its reports and nobody notices"; "Burnout claims fall in pilot sector". **New metric:** share of workers with a protected, uninterrupted rest period each week, and share who took a rest-cycle break in the last seven years.
- **First moves this quarter:** find one recurring task or report in your area and stop it for a month to see who notices; check whether portable long-service leave covers the sector you care about (`[to confirm]` which sectors have schemes).

### Stack D: the feast-day calendar
- **Metaphor:** the year is broken by shared days that mean something, like the holy days of a premodern calendar. The fourth free day is not a blank; it has a purpose the community gives it.
- **Worldview:** a premodern, partly Christian reading. Time is sacred in places, not uniform. A working year needs rhythm and marked days, and people are formed by what they celebrate together.
- **Systemic levers:** a set of new shared days tied to purposes (civic, seasonal, cultural), rather than a uniform fifth day; recognition of cultural and ceremonial leave for many traditions, including First Nations, in the NES; seasonal hours for industries with natural peaks (agriculture, tourism, retail); a public calendar process where communities propose days; employer reporting on days off that are shared versus scattered.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Harvest week becomes a national shared break"; "Workers can now take ceremonial leave in any tradition"; "The long weekend is out, the marked week is in". **New metric:** number of shared, purpose-named days per year that most workers actually have off together.
- **First moves this quarter:** list the shared days your team already keeps, and what each one is for; propose one purpose-named day to your team or community and see who joins.

## Reflection
All four stacks agree that the "100:80:100" trade is too narrow: each one refuses to make rest pay for itself in output. They split on who owns the time. The village well and the feast-day calendar give it to the community, the loom gives it to the person, and the fallow field gives it to a rule above both. That split matters for policy: the loom points to flexible individual rights, which suit knowledge workers most, while the well and the calendar need coordination, which suits frontline and regional workers but asks more of government. The fallow field is the only stack that directly protects casuals.
