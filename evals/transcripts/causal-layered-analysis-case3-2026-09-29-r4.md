## Reconstructed stacks

### Stack A: the fallow field
- **Metaphor:** a field that yields well because it rests in a planned season.
- **Worldview:** work follows seasons. Exhaustion is what happens to a field farmed every season, and a fallow period is part of farming, not a break from it.
- **Systemic levers:** mark two fallow periods in the calendar each year before the busy work is scheduled; agree a low-load week with your manager after each major delivery; rotate high-output projects with lower-intensity learning work; do a monthly soil test of sleep, mood and energy; ask the team to plan recovery gaps between projects the way it plans releases.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I took my fallow month and came back to my best quarter"; "The team plans recovery weeks the same way it plans releases". **New metric:** weeks of planned low load per year, taken as planned.
- **First moves this quarter:** map the last two years of work into seasons and mark where each burnout fell; book one low-load week after your next deadline; tell your manager the plan before the deadline.

### Stack B: the kinship circle
- **Metaphor:** a circle where each person carries part of the load and is carried in turn.
- **Worldview:** Ubuntu. A person is a person through other persons, so burnout is a sign that the circle has thinned or runs one way.
- **Systemic levers:** write the uncounted care work into your role description and your review; make sure every critical task has a second person who can do it; make asking for help a normal team practice; build a peer circle outside your reporting line that meets monthly; practise being carried, by accepting offers of help.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Our team counts care work in reviews"; "I asked for help and nothing bad happened". **New metric:** times a month you ask for or accept help.
- **First moves this quarter:** list the unseen work you do in one week; hand one task to a colleague with a proper handover; set up one monthly peer check-in.

### Stack C: pilgrimage with Sabbath stops
- **Metaphor:** a long walk with a set day of rest each week, where the walk was never a race.
- **Worldview:** Christian grace. Worth is given, not earned by output, and resting is trust that the world runs for a day without you. A secular reader can take the same practice without the theology.
- **Systemic levers:** one protected day a week with work notifications off; a weekly review that asks "what was enough?" in place of "what is undone?"; a contemplative practice such as prayer, silence or a walk; a team agreement on after-hours messages; a community outside work, faith-based or not, that knows you apart from your job.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "My phone stays off on Sundays and nothing broke"; "Our leader takes the day of rest and says so". **New metric:** protected rest days kept per month.
- **First moves this quarter:** choose one day and turn off work notifications on it for four weeks; each week, write down what happened at work on that day; run the "what was enough?" review each Friday.

### Stack D: the author who edits
- **Metaphor:** your working life as a manuscript that you write and edit, where cutting is part of the craft.
- **Worldview:** self-authorship. You choose what story work plays in your life, and each yes is a sentence you write into it.
- **Systemic levers:** a written "stop doing" list each quarter; a one-paragraph definition of "enough" for your role; a prepared script for saying no; a review at the one-year mark of whether this role and this pace are the story you want over two years; work with a coach or a qualified professional on any inner critic you find `[to confirm]`.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I cut three commitments and my best work got better"; "I turned down the role that would have repeated the pattern". **New metric:** commitments dropped per quarter.
- **First moves this quarter:** write a stop-doing list of three items; draft your "enough" paragraph; say no once, using the script.
