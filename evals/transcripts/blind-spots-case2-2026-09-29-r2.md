### Possible biases and fallacies

**False cause (fallacy), high confidence**
- Passage: "Our competitor's app went viral a month after they added dark mode, so dark mode drives growth"
- Generous reading: you may know that the competitor's viral moment was about dark mode, for example if the posts and reviews that spread it talked about dark mode. If so, the link is shown and the reason is sound for that competitor.
- Concern: this passage may treat one event that followed another as proof that the first caused the second.
- Checking question: What else changed at the competitor in that month, such as a campaign, a price change, another feature or a mention by a well-known account?

**False consensus effect (bias), medium confidence**
- Passage: "Everyone on my team uses dark mode"
- Generous reading: this may be offered as evidence that dark mode is widely liked among people who use apps heavily. That is sound if your customers look like your team.
- Concern: this passage may treat the preferences of the people building the app as the preferences of the people using it.
- Checking question: Have we asked people outside our own team, and do our customers use their devices the way we do?

**Sunk cost (bias), medium confidence**
- Passage: "We have already spent six months on the redesign, so we cannot pause it now"
- Generous reading: you may mean that pausing now has real future costs, such as an idle team, a missed release date or lost momentum. If so, the reason looks forward and is sound.
- Concern: this passage may rest the decision on time already spent rather than on what is still to come. It also argues against pausing the redesign, which is a separate question from whether dark mode belongs in it.
- Checking question: If we were starting today, would we add dark mode to this release, or ship the redesign first and add dark mode after?
