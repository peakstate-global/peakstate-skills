## Reconstructed stacks

### Stack A: the fallow field
- **Metaphor:** a field that yields well because it rests one season in seven.
- **Worldview:** rest is part of the work cycle and is commanded, not earned. A year without a fallow season is a planning error, not a sign of commitment.
- **Systemic levers:** keep one full day a week with no work contact; book a fallow week each quarter before targets are set; ask whoever sets your workload `[to confirm]` to plan the year with named low seasons; take leave in blocks rather than scattered days (check your leave terms `[to confirm]`); track recovery days beside output.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I booked my fallow week before the plan, not after it"; "The quiet month was my best month for ideas"; "My team plans the low season first." **New metric:** fallow days taken per quarter.
- **First moves this quarter:** keep one work-free day a week for 12 weeks; put one fallow week in the calendar now; score your energy out of 10 each Friday.

### Stack B: the village fire
- **Metaphor:** a fire the village keeps together, so no one tends it alone all night.
- **Worldview:** Ubuntu. Energy is a shared resource, and carrying it alone is the fault to fix, not a strength to admire.
- **Systemic levers:** list the tasks only you can do and hand one over; share after-hours cover on a rota; ask for help at the first sign of load, not the last; set a monthly load check with two peers; make team workload visible in one place.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I asked for help in week one, not week ten"; "Our team shares the late watch"; "Nobody here is the only one who can." **New metric:** number of tasks only you can do (aim for fewer).
- **First moves this quarter:** write the list of tasks only you can do; hand one over; book the first peer load check.

### Stack C: the apprentice
- **Metaphor:** an apprentice who learns the pace of the craft from a master, not only the skill.
- **Worldview:** self-determination through mastery. You author your working life by treating a sustainable pace as a skill you practise.
- **Systemic levers:** find one person who does similar work without burning out, and study how `[to confirm who]`; write your early warning signs; define "done" before a task starts; keep a short weekly log of pace; ask for a mentor or coach on pace, not output.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I learned the pace, not only the job"; "I now see my limit two days before I hit it"; "I stop at done, not at exhausted." **New metric:** warning signs caught before a crash.
- **First moves this quarter:** ask one sustainable colleague how they pace their work; write your first three warning signs; define done for your next three tasks.

### Stack D: kintsugi
- **Metaphor:** a bowl mended with gold along the crack, where the repair adds to its value.
- **Worldview:** sufficiency. A working life does not need to be unbroken or maximal, and "enough" is a measure you set, not a limit you apologise for.
- **Systemic levers:** set a line for "enough" each Monday; drop one standing commitment; judge each quarter by what you kept whole as well as by output; tell one trusted person about the pattern, if you want to `[to confirm]`; talk to a doctor or other qualified professional if your health is affected.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I stopped hiding the crack"; "Enough was the target this quarter, and I hit it"; "The mend made me better at the work." **New metric:** weeks in which you reached "enough" and stopped.
- **First moves this quarter:** write the "enough" line each Monday; drop one commitment; if burnout is affecting your health, book a doctor.
