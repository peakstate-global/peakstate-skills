## Reconstructed stacks

### Stack A: fallow field
- **Metaphor:** a field that grows well for years because it rests between crops.
- **Worldview:** capacity regenerates in cycles. A field cropped every season fails, however good the farmer. The rest period is part of the harvest.
- **Systemic levers:** plan the year in seasons, with a named fallow period after each big delivery; book the fallow period before the peak, not after the crash; rotate crops by alternating one high-intensity project with a maintenance project; take on no new commitments during a fallow period; test the soil once a month with a short self-check on sleep, energy and mood.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "He turns down a third project: that field is fallow until March"; "The two-yearly burnout cycle breaks after the first planned fallow"; "The team plans quarters with a fallow fortnight". **New metric:** planned fallow weeks per year, against unplanned crash days.
- **First moves this quarter:** mark the next fallow period in your calendar now; list your current work as crops and name one to rotate out.

### Stack B: kinship fire
- **Metaphor:** a fire that everyone tends, so no single person has to keep it alight.
- **Worldview:** Ubuntu. You are a person through other people. Burnout shows that you carried alone something that should be carried together.
- **Systemic levers:** make your capacity a team conversation instead of a private struggle; pair on key work so the load is visible; set a trigger for asking for help, such as a second late night in one week; name two people who can spot your early signs `[to confirm who]`; keep a standing weekly time with kin or friends that work cannot move; tend other people's fires too, so help flows both ways.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "The team sees the overload in week two, not week ten"; "He asks for help before the crash, for the first time"; "Workload is set in the open". **New metric:** times per month you ask for or accept help.
- **First moves this quarter:** tell two people your early warning signs; book the first standing time with kin or friends.

### Stack C: the seventh day
- **Metaphor:** one day in seven that work cannot enter.
- **Worldview:** sabbath. Rest is the goal of the week and is holy by design. You stop because the week is complete, not because you are empty.
- **Systemic levers:** keep one full stop day each week with no work devices; use a start ritual and an end ritual so the boundary feels real; write down what "enough" means for a done week; add a nightly stop time as a small sabbath each evening; within the two years, plan one longer break on the sabbatical idea.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Saturday sealed: phone in a drawer for 18 months running"; "He finishes the week, instead of the week finishing him". **New metric:** full stop days per month.
- **First moves this quarter:** choose the day; put your devices away for one full 24 hours and note what comes up.

### Stack D: kintsugi
- **Metaphor:** a bowl mended in gold, more valuable because the breaks are visible.
- **Worldview:** self-authorship. You write your life from your own values. The breaks become part of the design, not something you hide and repeat.
- **Systemic levers:** write your own account of the last burnout (what cracked and what led up to it); turn each lesson into one personal rule; tell your manager your pattern, if you judge the risk worth it; choose work by your values, not by what proves you; renegotiate the scope of your role from that account.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "The one who knows his cracks, and plans around them"; "The same warning sign, a different response this time". **New metric:** early warning signs you act on within one week.
- **First moves this quarter:** write a one-page map of the last burnout; choose one rule to keep for the next 90 days.
