## Reconstructed stacks

### Stack A: Fallow field
- **Metaphor:** a field that grows well because it rests in turn.
- **Worldview:** effort and rest are one cycle. A year with no winter is extractive, and your output over two years depends on the fallow seasons in it.
- **Systemic levers:** plan the next two years in seasons, each with a named fallow block; after each big push, a fallow week with no new commitments; rotate "crops" by alternating intense projects with maintenance work; agree the season plan with whoever sets your workload; keep a written list of your own early signs of depletion `[to confirm what they are]`.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I took my fallow week and nothing burned down"; "You planned the year in four seasons, not twelve sprints"; "My second year was calmer than my first, and I delivered more". **New metric:** fallow days taken against fallow days planned, per quarter.
- **First moves this quarter:** name the season you are in now and when it ends; put one fallow block in the next 90 days; write down the early signs you noticed before past burnouts.

### Stack B: The Sabbath table
- **Metaphor:** a table you arrive at each week, whatever the week held.
- **Worldview:** rest is not earned by work. Worth is given, not proved. Rest is the rhythm that makes work good.
- **Systemic levers:** one fixed day a week fully off work, devices included; a shared meal or time with people on that day; a short stop ritual at the end of the working week; a standing rule that requests on that day wait; review the week before the day off, not after it.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Week 12, and I still haven't opened my laptop on my day off"; "You stopped apologising for being unreachable on your day off"; "The stop ritual is the part of the week I look forward to". **New metric:** consecutive weeks with one full day off.
- **First moves this quarter:** choose the day; tell the people who need to know; take work apps off your phone for that day.

### Stack C: The village well
- **Metaphor:** a well the whole village draws from and keeps up.
- **Worldview:** I am because we are. A load carried alone is a design fault, not a virtue. Asking for help is a contribution to the village.
- **Systemic levers:** map who draws on you and who you draw from; list the tasks only you can do, and shrink that list; pair with a colleague on work you carry alone; teach others what only you know; form a small peer circle that checks in on load; ask for help before you reach capacity, not after.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "The team ran fine while I was away"; "You asked for help on Tuesday, not on the night before the deadline"; "Three people can now do what only I could do". **New metric:** number of tasks only you can do, falling each quarter.
- **First moves this quarter:** write the list of tasks only you can do; hand one of them to someone else; name two people who will check in with you on load.

### Stack D: Weaver who ties off each piece
- **Metaphor:** a weaver who finishes each piece, ties it off, and then chooses the next one.
- **Worldview:** your working life is a work you make. Worth is in the quality and completion of pieces, not in the volume of thread.
- **Systemic levers:** a fixed number of open commitments at once; a tie-off ritual at the end of each project; choose new work by how it fits the pattern you want; decline work that does not fit; design your week before others fill it.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "I finished three things this quarter instead of starting ten"; "You said no to a good project because it didn't fit the pattern"; "Every piece I finished this year, I'd sign". **New metric:** number of open commitments at any one time.
- **First moves this quarter:** count your open commitments; set a limit; close or hand back two.

## Reflection
All four stacks agree that the redline engine has no built-in stop, and each one adds a stop of a different kind. The fallow field and the Sabbath table add the stop in time: the field works on a scale of seasons, the table on a scale of weeks. The weaver adds the stop in scope: fewer pieces, each one finished. The village well is the odd one out, because it adds no stop for you alone. It says the load was never meant to sit on one person. That split matters: if the pattern lives in your own calendar, A, B and D will help, and if it lives in the role you hold for others `[to confirm]`, only C reaches it.
