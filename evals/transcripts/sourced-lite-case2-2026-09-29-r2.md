## Final position

The claim, as written, fails on the best evidence I could find: in two field studies of whole floors moved to open plan, face-to-face interaction fell by about 70%, and email and instant messaging rose. ## Adversarial findings

- **Strongest case against the conclusion:** the proposal's central reason is the claim the best study was designed to test, and that study found the opposite, at firms that moved for exactly this reason. Bernstein and Turban also report that OpenCo1 executives told them productivity, by their own internal metrics, "had declined after the redesign" (section 2, "Performance outcome"). A manager who has read this study, and it was widely covered, will raise it.
- **Against my own research:** both Bernstein and Turban studies were Fortune 500 headquarters, whole floors, with mixed functions and leadership. A single small team moved together into its own area is a different case, and I found no study of that case in this session. The productivity point is a qualitative report from management, not a measured result. Kim and de Dear measure satisfaction, not behaviour.
- **A better explanation for the same facts:** open plan does not remove the need for boundaries, it moves them into behaviour. People build invisible walls (C4), so the talk you hoped the missing walls would allow goes into chat instead.
- **C2 would be false if:** a replication with badges or similar sensors found face-to-face time rising, or flat, after a comparable move.
- **C4 would be false if:** open-plan teams showed no rise in headphone use, messaging to nearby colleagues, or reported interruption avoidance.
- **C1 fails.** Holds: possibly where the move shortens large distances between people who depend on each other, for example a team spread over several floors brought onto one (this rests on C5, which is recalled). Fails: in the two measured cases where people already sat on the same floor and walls were removed, where face-to-face time fell about 70%. Instead: in those cases people swapped talk for email and messaging.
- **C3 fails.** Holds: occupants rate ease of interaction as a small positive of open plan. Fails: that benefit is no larger than in private offices and is outweighed by noise and loss of privacy. Instead: the net effect on workspace satisfaction is negative.

## Integration

This is a conditional. Open plan can raise face-to-face contact in one region: where the move cuts real distance between people who depend on each other and who are far apart today. Outside that region, the measured result is less talk and more messaging. The observation that places your team: today, do people who need each other sit on different floors or in separate rooms, or are they already within a short walk? If they are already close, the move will likely not deliver the benefit and may reduce it.
