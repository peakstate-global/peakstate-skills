## Reconstructed stacks

### Stack A: fallow field
- **Metaphor:** a field that gives a good yield over years because it is left to rest between crops.
- **Worldview:** work has seasons. Output depends on the soil, and the soil needs fallow time. Worth is measured over a year, not a week.
- **Systemic levers:** plan the year in seasons, with fallow weeks named in advance; after each big push, a fixed fallow period before the next commitment; ask your manager `[to confirm]` to plan intense projects in rotation, not back to back; judge yourself by yield over a season, not by weekly output; one fallow day each week with no goal to produce anything.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Finished the push, took the fallow fortnight as planned"; "My calendar shows seasons, not only deadlines"; "Started the next project rested". **New metric:** fallow days taken after each peak period, against plan.
- **First moves this quarter:** mark the peaks of the last 12 months that you can recall; book one fallow week after the next known peak; tell one person at work that it is booked.

### Stack B: canoe crew
- **Metaphor:** a crew that paddles in time and swaps seats before anyone is spent.
- **Worldview:** Ubuntu. Burnout is one paddler pulling alone. Strength belongs to the crew, and asking for relief is part of good paddling.
- **Systemic levers:** list who shares each load you carry; agree a team norm for handing a load on when someone is near their limit; treat asking for help early as a skill; rotate the lead seat on hard projects; count the crew's capacity, not one person's heroics; a weekly check with one peer.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "The team swapped seats before anyone hit the wall"; "Asking for cover counted as good teamwork"; "No single name on every late-night message". **New metric:** times each week you hand a load on or ask for help.
- **First moves this quarter:** list the loads you carry alone; ask one colleague to share one of them; propose a handover norm at a team meeting.

### Stack C: Sabbath palace in time
- **Metaphor:** a set period each week that work does not enter, like a room with a locked door.
- **Worldview:** rest is not earned by finishing. It is a boundary that holds whatever state the work is in, and keeping it is a form of freedom.
- **Systemic levers:** a fixed weekly stop time, kept even when work is unfinished; work apps off the phone for that window; a short written list of what counts as a true emergency; tell your team the boundary once, plainly; a small ritual to open and close the rest period.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Work stopped at the set hour, unfinished, and the week went fine"; "Nobody noticed the missing Saturday emails"; "The rest day is in the calendar like a meeting". **New metric:** weeks each quarter the full rest period was kept.
- **First moves this quarter:** pick the day and the start and end times; remove work apps for that window; write the emergency list and share it.

### Stack D: kintsugi
- **Metaphor:** a bowl repaired with gold, so the cracks are the most visible and most valued part.
- **Worldview:** the plural self. Past burnouts are part of the story, not failures to hide. A life with several centres does not break when one of them is strained.
- **Systemic levers:** write down each past burnout plainly, and what came first each time `[to confirm]`; name your early warning signs; tell one trusted person at work what those signs are; build one part of life that output does not measure; choose roles and projects that fit the repaired shape, not the old one.
- **New litany (illustrative):** "Told my manager what my early warning signs look like"; "Spotted the pattern in week one, not month three"; "The part of my life that isn't work has a weekly slot". **New metric:** early warning signs noticed and acted on within one week.
- **First moves this quarter:** write the pattern of past burnouts on one page; pick the one early sign you see first; tell one trusted person.
