### Possible biases and fallacies

**False dilemma (fallacy), high confidence**
- Passage: "Either we protect our businesses or we hand the street to cyclists."
- Generous reading: if Main Street is too narrow for both a lane and the parking or loading the shops need, the councillor may be naming a real trade-off in sharp words. In that case the choice is real, even if the wording is loaded.
- Concern: this passage may present two options when more exist, such as a lane that keeps loading bays, a lane on a parallel street, or a trial period.
- Checking question: What third option has not been named, and has council looked at any design that keeps parking and adds a lane?

**False cause (fallacy), medium confidence**
- Passage: "Bike lanes on Main Street will kill local shops. Since the Smith Road lane opened, two cafes on Smith Road have closed."
- Generous reading: "since" may only mean "after", offered as a warning sign. If the cafes' owners said the lane was why they closed, or trade fell across the whole street after it opened, the link would be sound.
- Concern: the text may treat a sequence as a cause. Cafes close for many reasons (rent, staff, the owner retiring), and the text does not rule any of them out.
- Checking question: What else changed on Smith Road at the same time, and what did the two cafes give as their reasons for closing?

**Hasty generalisation (fallacy), medium confidence**
- Passage: "two cafes on Smith Road have closed" offered for "Bike lanes on Main Street will kill local shops"
- Generous reading: if Smith Road had only a handful of shops, two closures is a large share and a fair warning.
- Concern: this passage may draw a rule for all local shops from two cases, on one street, of one kind of business.
- Checking question: How many shops are on Smith Road, and how many closed in the same period before the lane opened?

**Appeal to authority (fallacy), medium confidence**
- Passage: "Every serious planner knows lanes hurt trade."

- Checking question: Which planners or reports, exactly, and do other planners in the same field agree?
