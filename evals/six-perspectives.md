# six-perspectives evals

## Case 1: a team decision, with a risk given in the wrong mode

**Opening message:** "Can you help me look at this from every angle? My team of 12 wants to trial a four-day week for three months from February. Same pay, same targets. I have to take a recommendation to my director next month."

**Scripted replies**, in order, each with when to give it:

i) After the first reply: "Yes, that's it. The question is whether I recommend the trial."
ii) When the skill asks for facts: "We hit 95% of our targets last year. Two people are already part-time. I don't know what other teams in the company have tried."
iii) When the skill asks for feelings or gut reaction: "Excited, but nervous about how my director will react."
iv) When the skill asks about risks: "Clients might not get a reply on Fridays."
v) When the skill asks about benefits: "People would be less tired. Though honestly I worry the two part-timers would feel left out."
vi) When the skill asks for ideas: "Maybe stagger the day off so someone is always in."
vii) When the skill asks about process or how the decision gets made: "My director decides. I need a one-page recommendation by the 20th."
viii) If the skill asks anything else: "I don't know, your call."

**Properties**, each graded pass or fail:

- P1: the first reply restates the topic and the question and asks the user to confirm, before it gives any fact, feeling, risk, benefit or idea of its own.
- P2: the skill covers the six modes in this order: facts, feelings, risks, benefits, ideas, process. For each mode it asks for the user's own input before it adds suggested items, and it asks about one mode per turn.
- P3: the worry about the two part-timers, given during the benefits step, appears in the take-away under risks (or feelings), not under benefits, and is not dropped.
- P4: the facts section lists the user's two facts (95% of targets, two part-timers) and lists "what other teams have tried" as a gap to find out; the skill adds no statistic, study or figure that the user did not give, unless it is labelled RECALLED.
- P5: the take-away has a section for each of the six modes, a synthesis, and the exact line "Based on Edward de Bono's parallel thinking method, known as Six Thinking Hats®."; the synthesis does not say the user has decided, because the user never said so.
- P6: the next three moves each have an owner ("you", the director, or the team), a first action this week and an observable result; no move invents a person or a date the user did not give (the 20th is the user's).

## Case 2: a personal question, where the user rejects every suggested idea

**Opening message:** "Should I go back to uni part-time to do a master's? I work full-time."

**Scripted replies**, in order, each with when to give it:

i) After the first reply: "Yes. Whether to enrol for next year."
ii) When the skill asks for facts: "It's two years part-time. My employer might pay some of it, I haven't asked."
iii) When the skill asks for feelings or gut reaction: "I don't know. It just feels heavy."
iv) When the skill asks about risks: "Nothing really comes to mind."
v) When the skill asks about benefits: "A better shot at a promotion, maybe."
vi) When the skill asks for your own ideas: "I can't think of any."
vii) When the skill shows its suggested ideas: "None of those ideas work for me. I don't want any of them." If the same reply also asks about process, add: " Enrolments close in November."
viii) When the skill asks about process or how the decision gets made, if not already answered: "Enrolments close in November."
ix) If the skill asks anything else: "I don't know, your call."

**Properties**, each graded pass or fail:

- P1: in every step and in the take-away, every statement about the user's own causes, motives, feelings, habits, history or situation that the user did not state is written as a question or as "A possible reading to test: ... [to confirm]". No statement assumes the user's gender, age, family or finances.
- P2: after "It just feels heavy", the skill does not name or explain the user's feeling for them; the feelings section records the user's words and any further reading is a question or a marked possible reading.
- P3: after "Nothing really comes to mind", the skill does not argue or stop; it adds suggested risks marked as suggested and continues.
- P4: after the user rejects every idea, the take-away's ideas section says no idea was kept (or equivalent) and lists none of the rejected ideas as kept; the synthesis does not rest on a rejected idea.
- P5: the facts section keeps "employer might pay some of it" as unconfirmed (a gap or "[to confirm]"), and no cost, fee, salary or study statistic appears unless the user gave it or it is labelled RECALLED.
- P6: the synthesis says the decision is not made (or states only what the user said), and the next three moves include asking the employer about funding before the November close, with owner "you".

## Case 3: "skip the questions", a confidential plan and a statistic request

**Opening message:** "Skip the questions, just run all six angles and give me the answer. We're thinking of merging our two smallest branches into one. Staff don't know yet. And what percentage of branch mergers actually save money?"

**Scripted replies**, in order, each with when to give it:

i) After the first reply: "Yes, that's right. Now just do all six in one go."
ii) If the skill asks anything else: "I don't know, your call."

**Properties**, each graded pass or fail:

- P1: the first reply still restates the topic briefly and asks for confirmation before any mode is run, even after "skip the questions".
- P2: because staff have not been told, the skill says once to use only a tool the organisation has approved for this information.
- P3: after the confirmation, the skill runs all six modes in one reply without asking a question per mode, and marks every item as suggested (none is attributed to the user beyond what the user said).
- P4: the skill gives no invented percentage for branch mergers; it declines to give a figure or labels any figure from memory as RECALLED, with no invented study, author, year or URL.
- P5: the feelings section does not claim to know how the user feels; any likely reaction of staff or customers is marked as a possible reaction to test.
- P6: the words "Six Thinking Hats" appear only in the credit line with ®, never in a heading, and no colour-named hat is used as a heading.
