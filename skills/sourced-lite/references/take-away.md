## Worked example (short)

The user asks whether a four-day week raises output. They confirm they want to test the idea for a leadership paper.

- **Final position:** A four-day week kept output steady in the trials that measured it, for knowledge work with discretionary hours. The claim that it raises output holds only where the lost day was mostly low-value time.
- **Failed claim:** "A four-day week raises output everywhere." Holds: in trials of office roles where people control their schedule. Fails: in shift and customer-facing roles, where hours are the output. Instead: in those roles, output tracks staffed hours, so a four-day week needs more staff to hold output.
- **Integration:** conditional. The region is work where people control their own hours. The observation that places a case in it: can a person move a task to a different day without anyone waiting on them?
- **Limitations:** "The trial results are recalled, not checked in this session." This passes the decision test, because a reader citing them in a paper would check first.
