# The arc sheet

Fill this in before any slide or paragraph is written, and get it approved.

## Template

**Audience:** who they are, what they already know, what they are responsible for.

**Job to be done:** When [situation], they want to [progress], so they can [outcome].

**Where they start:**

| Belief they arrive with | What it costs them | OBSERVED or INFERRED | Section that answers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| … | … | … | … |

**Pains to name:** the frustrations the piece must acknowledge, even where it does not solve them.

**The shift:** from "[what they would say now]" to "[what they can say at the end]".

**The emotional journey:** they arrive feeling [wary, cynical, anxious…] and leave feeling [hopeful, resolved…]. The understanding moment, where their concerns are stated fairly before any promise, is section [n].

**Where to start:** the one action they take afterwards.

**The spine:**

| Section | Its premise, in one sentence | Moves them from saying | To saying | They feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | … | … | … | … |

Rules for the spine:

- The "to saying" of each section is the "from saying" of the next.
- Vocabulary comes before application: teach the options before the audience applies them to its own case.
- The last section answers "where do I start?", not "what is the method?".
- Read the premises alone, top to bottom. If they do not make the argument, the spine is not finished.
- Read the feelings alone, top to bottom. If the room is asked to hope before it feels understood, move the understanding section earlier.

## Worked example: a 90 minute workshop on where AI belongs in the work

**Audience:** internal agile and product coaches in a large organisation. They use a desktop assistant, hear "agents" everywhere, and are asked by teams and executives which work AI should take.

**Job to be done:** When a team or executive asks what to automate, they want a way to choose, so they can give an answer they can defend and start somewhere safe.

**Where they start:**

| Belief they arrive with | What it costs them | Label | Section |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI means a chatbot | They miss the plays already running, such as scoring models | INFERRED | 2 |
| The question is which jobs AI replaces | Roles are bundles of work, so the question has no answer | OBSERVED | 3 |
| Agents are the destination | Ideas go to the most expensive, least predictable play, when a workflow often returns more | OBSERVED | 2 and 6 |
| We have to be seen doing AI | Pilot sprawl, pilots that return nothing, and fatigue | INFERRED | 5 |
| A human in the loop makes it safe | The person at the gate cannot keep up, so it becomes a rubber stamp or a chokepoint | OBSERVED | 6 |

**The shift:** from "AI is a chatbot, and we should find processes it can take over" to "AI is four different plays, we choose the work first, kill most ideas cheaply, and design what the human at the gate is for".

**Where to start:** one bounded, checkable workflow step that repeats every week, with a named person at the gate.

**The spine:**

| Section | Premise | From saying | To saying |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Open | You hold beliefs about AI that are costing you | "This is one more AI session." | "I recognise one of these beliefs." |
| 2. What AI is now | AI is four plays that differ in who holds the loop, cost and return | "Agents are where it is heading." | "The most autonomous play is the most expensive, not the most valuable." |
| 3. Which work | The unit is the work, never the job | "Which jobs will AI replace?" | "I can place my own work and name its posture." |
| 4. Match | Each posture reaches for different plays | "Any AI will do." | "I know which plays fit my work." |
| 5. Kill it cheaply | Most candidates should die in the room | "We should start a pilot." | "My candidate survives five questions, or I saved a quarter." |
| 6. Price it honestly | The person at the gate sets the ceiling | "A human in the loop makes it safe." | "I know what my gate's person is for, and whether they can keep up." |
| 7. Start here | Start one rung lower, with a named gate and a date | "Where do I start?" | "I leave with one candidate, its play, its gate and a date." |
